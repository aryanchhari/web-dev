THE BIG PICTURE

Small details. Big differences.

01

One pixel, many parts.

Red, green and blue subpixels combine to produce colours. The exact subpixel arrangement depends on the display.

02

Black is a useful test.

An OLED pixel can stop emitting light. A conventional LCD still has a backlight behind its dark areas.

03

Mini LED is still LCD.

Mini LED describes a backlight approach. Dimming zones help control light behind groups of LCD pixels.

LESS JARGON. MORE CLARITY.

A pocket display dictionary.

The number of pixels in an image. A 2560 × 1440 display has 2560 pixels across and 1440 down. Sharpness also depends on screen size and viewing distance.

How often a display refreshes each second, measured in hertz. 144 Hz means 144 refreshes per second.

The time a pixel takes to change between levels or colours. It is different from refresh rate and from the total delay between an input and its visible result.

The difference between bright and dark parts of an image. Room light, reflections and local dimming also affect what you perceive.
